{{ icon("leaf", 16) }} Offline leaf check for coffee farmers

Diagnose, track, warn.

Jirani checks three coffee leaves on a phone the family already has, with no internet. It says what is likely wrong, or says "not sure, ask a person". The result goes to a basic phone, and the cooperative can warn neighbouring farms.

The problem

Yields are falling, and expert advice is far away

A smallholder coffee farmer whose yields are dropping has no quick, trustworthy way to find out what is wrong with her trees, and no way to warn her neighbours. She may not own a smartphone or have internet.

About 800,000 smallholders grow Kenya's coffee, in about 500 cooperative societies. International Coffee Organization, Country Coffee Profile: Kenya, 2019
Kenya's extension policy says the ratio of extension staff to farmers "has not improved". Its target is one officer per 600 farmers by 2029. Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock Development, Agricultural Sector Extension Policy, 2023
61% of women in low- and middle-income countries own a smartphone. About 945 million do not. GSMA, Mobile Gender Gap Report 2025
How it works

Built around the phone that is actually there

The household smartphone is home only some of the time, and there is no Wi-Fi. So the sample comes to the phone, and the cooperative is the hub.

1

Leaves and cherry come home

Three leaves and a handful of cherry on the printed card. The cooperative prints the capture card (PDF): three leaf boxes, a cherry box and a 20 mm scale bar.

2

The phone checks them offline

A small model on the phone reads the photos. It answers only when it is confident and at least two leaves agree.

3

The result reaches a basic phone

Daughter's phone, Sunday: the result is copied to the basic phone as one prefilled SMS, which the farmer sends herself, plus spoken Kiswahili audio.

4

The cooperative warns neighbours

Reports sync when there is signal. Clusters become draft alerts, and an officer approves each one.

What it does

One card, fixed slots

{{ icon("scan", 24) }}

Offline diagnosis that can refuse

Leaf miner, leaf rust, brown leaf spot, cercospora or healthy. If the photos are poor or the leaves disagree, the only answer is "Not sure. Ask a person. Do not spray."

{{ icon("trend", 24) }}

Severity and trend

How bad it is, and whether the block is worse, the same or better than the last check within six weeks.

{{ icon("layers", 24) }}

Where it looked

A heatmap on each leaf shows the area the model used, so a person can judge the result.

{{ icon("cloud", 24) }}

Likely causes of the yield drop

Transparent rules combine the leaf result with cached rainfall and soil estimates, and say what the tool cannot see.

{{ icon("volume", 24) }}

Handoff to a basic phone

A single-part SMS of 160 characters or fewer, and Kiswahili audio built from pre-recorded clips.

{{ icon("bell", 24) }}

Neighbour early warning

Three or more plots within 2 km reporting the same problem in 14 days create a draft alert on the dashboard.

{{ icon("pin", 24) }}

Visits in the right order

Severe or worsening blocks become visit tickets, ranked so the officer knows where to go first. Once the officer confirms a date, the farmer sees when the visit is and what to do until then.

{{ icon("list", 24) }}

Cherry band and what buyers paid

An optional cherry photo gets band A, B or C from a prototype colour check, not a trained model. Next to it the card shows the last three cooperative buyer tickets for that band as a range. It is never a price offer, and the photo never produces a price.

{{ icon("users", 24) }}

Home language, phrase-locked

The three questions can be played as recorded Gikuyu prompts, then shown in Kiswahili. Gikuyu is limited to a fixed list of human recordings because generated Gikuyu would hallucinate. Any other language gets the picture card only.

{{ icon("camera", 24) }}

Ask the officer, without a farm visit

If she chooses, the farmer sends the three leaf photos. The officer looks at them on the dashboard and replies from a fixed list.

Guardrails and limits

What it will not do, and what is not proven

{{ icon("shield", 24) }}

Guardrails

  • {{ icon("check") }}A person decides. The farmer presses Send. Alerts are drafts until an officer approves them.
  • {{ icon("check") }}Photos stay on the phone unless the farmer asks. A report carries the plot, block and result only. Leaf photos go to the officer only when the farmer switches on "Ask the extension officer".
  • {{ icon("check") }}No chemicals. No product names and no doses, checked by an automated test.
  • {{ icon("check") }}Fixed answers only. No language model runs anywhere in the app or the server.
{{ icon("alert", 24) }}

Known limits

  • {{ icon("alert") }}Trained on Brazilian leaves. Accuracy on Kenyan varieties and field conditions is unknown.
  • {{ icon("alert") }}Machine-drafted Kiswahili until human clips land. The Kiswahili text and the result clips are machine-drafted. The clips come from Meta MMS-TTS, a non-commercial (CC-BY-NC) model; audio for judging should be human recordings with the same file names. The advice text is not reviewed by an agronomist.
  • {{ icon("alert") }}Gikuyu clips are placeholders. Gikuyu is phrase-locked to a fixed list of recorded prompts, and none has been recorded yet. Until then the questions are answered with taps.
  • {{ icon("alert") }}Cherry band is a prototype. A colour check, not a trained model, and not tested on Kenyan cherry. The card says "Confirm at the factory".
  • {{ icon("alert") }}Synthetic demo data. The cooperative is fictional; its {{ n_plots }} plot points are random points in a real coffee-growing area{% if area %} ({{ area|replace("Coffee-growing area near ", "near ") }}){% endif %}. The buyer tickets are synthetic too.
  • {{ icon("alert") }}Mock SMS. Neighbour alerts are recorded as "would be sent"; no real message goes out.
