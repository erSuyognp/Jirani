## BRACOL: what we actually found (inspected 2026-10-03)

- **Leaf side and setting:** photos of the **abaxial (lower) side** of single detached leaves on a **white background**, taken with five smartphone models (ASUS Zenfone 2, Xiaomi Redmi 5A, Xiaomi S2, Galaxy S8, iPhone 6S). Images are 2048×1024 landscape with the leaf lying horizontally.
- **Origin:** Santa Maria de Marechal Floreano, mountain region of Espirito Santo, Brazil.
- **Label file:** `leaf/dataset.csv`, columns `id, predominant_stress, miner, rust, phoma, cercospora, severity`.
- **Stress codes:** 0 healthy, 1 leaf miner, 2 leaf rust, 3 phoma (brown leaf spot), 4 cercospora leaf spot. Mapping confirmed from the authors' code ([esgario/lara2018](https://github.com/esgario/lara2018), symptom folders `1_health … 5_cercospora`).
- **Code 5 (62 rows):** not documented. These rows have mixed or no secondary stress flags, and the authors' own `dataset.csv` (1,685 rows) excludes them. We exclude them too. `TODO: verify` the meaning of code 5 with the authors.
- **Severity codes** (from the dataset description, % of leaf area with symptoms): 0 healthy (< 0.1%), 1 very low (0.1–5%), 2 low (5–10%), 3 high (10–15%), 4 very high (> 15%).
- **Split:** the dataset ships no split files. The authors split in code (numpy seed 150 shuffle, fold 1, 70/15/15). `ml/prepare_data.py` reproduces that split exactly and saves it to `ml/data/splits/`.
- **Truncated archive:** the published Mendeley zip (`BRACOL_coffee_leaf_ images_datasets.zip`, 164,516,964 bytes, sha256 `25a2fc97…fdfc1f`, matching Mendeley's own hash) ends partway through. Only 1,402 of 1,747 leaf images extract, the last one is cut off, and the symptom dataset is absent. After dropping code 5 and missing files we have **1,342 usable images**:

| Split | n | healthy | miner | rust | phoma | cercospora |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 934 | 92 | 182 | 332 | 238 | 90 |
| val | 200 | 27 | 31 | 69 | 48 | 25 |
| test | 208 | 23 | 40 | 64 | 60 | 21 |

Severity (train / val / test): healthy 92/27/23, very low 565/110/118, low 184/49/42, high 60/8/15, very high 33/6/10.

The full image set is in the authors' GitHub repo (MIT-licensed code; images are the same BRACOL images). `python ml/prepare_data.py --fetch-missing` fills the gap from there. Our reported results use only the images that were present locally unless the report says otherwise.

Stress × severity: healthy is always severity 0 and diseased leaves are never 0. Miner never reaches "very high", and cercospora is almost all "very low" (118 of 136).

## What the data does not cover

- BRACOL is **Brazilian**. It does not include Kenyan or East African varieties (for example SL28, SL34, Ruiru 11) or highland Kenyan field conditions.
- **No nutrient-deficiency class.** Yellowing from nitrogen deficiency is not a trained label and should fall through to "not sure". This is not guaranteed: the model can still be confidently wrong on unseen conditions.
- Images are **single detached leaves on a white background**, photographed from the lower side. They are not leaves on the tree, not cluttered backgrounds, and not the upper side.
- **No abiotic stress labels** (drought, frost, sunscald).
- Several biotic stresses can co-occur on one leaf, but the label records only the predominant one.
- Severity classes are imbalanced: few "high" or "very high" examples, and almost none for cercospora.
- The cooperative registry, plot coordinates, and outbreak reports in the demo are **synthetic**.
- Rainfall and soil context come from **coarse global grids**, not plot-level measurement.
